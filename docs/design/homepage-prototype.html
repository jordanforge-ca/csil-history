The central idea

A progression in where support — and authority — sits

The surviving evidence does not show that one project formally became the next. It does show a progression of ideas that changed what publicly funded home support could look like.

A model with continuing relevance

More than three decades of experience with person-directed, publicly funded care

CSIL demonstrates that publicly funded home support does not always have to be institutionally managed. It can give the person receiving support — and, where necessary, representatives or support groups — a much greater role in organizing that care.

That experience is worth understanding as British Columbia considers how more people can be supported safely at home.
